# GROUNDWORK — Brand Strategy

Client site for a performance & recovery specialist for runners, built on the real
professional background of **Melika Ghali** (Registered Massage Therapist, NCAA
Division I distance runner — Boise State & Eastern Michigan — MS Exercise
Physiology, currently practicing in Toronto). Public bio sources: The Runner's
Academy, LiveActive Sport Medicine, Myodetox.

Positioning note: the founder is branded as a **performance & recovery
specialist for runners** rather than "massage therapist" — that umbrella
naturally covers massage, strength coaching, and running coaching, and scales
into products, memberships, and education without ever needing to be
re-branded later.

## 1. Brand Name — Selected

**GROUNDWORK**
*(full lock-up: "Groundwork Performance" when a descriptor is needed)*

Rationale: "groundwork" is literally what a strength-first, recovery-first
running practice is built on — foundational work most runners skip. It reads
as serious and scientific without being clinical, is short enough to work as
a wordmark and a URL, and isn't tied to one person's name, so it can grow
into a multi-practitioner or licensable brand later.

## 2. Brand Positioning

For competitive and recreational runners who treat training as something to
survive rather than build, **Groundwork** is the performance and recovery
practice that treats strength, massage, and coaching as one system —
because unlike single-discipline coaches or clinics, Groundwork is run by a
practitioner who has raced NCAA Division I and now treats runners for a
living, so the plan on the whiteboard and the hands on the table come from
the same person.

## Mission Statement

To keep runners running — longer careers, fewer injuries, better
performances — by treating strength and recovery as training, not an
afterthought.

## Core Values

1. **Evidence over trends** — training and treatment decisions trace back to
   sports science, not whatever is popular on social media this month.
2. **Recovery is performance** — adaptation happens after the session, not
   during it.
3. **Strength is the foundation** — durable runners are built in the gym
   before they're built on the road.
4. **Longevity over quick fixes** — the goal is decades of running, not one
   good block.
5. **Earned trust** — direct, honest communication; no overselling a
   program or a product.

## Voice & Tone

- **Confident, not hype-y.** States what works and why. Avoids exclamation
  points and superlatives.
- **Precise.** Uses real terminology (tissue, tempo, threshold, eccentric)
  without over-explaining to the point of condescension.
- **Warm under the discipline.** This is still a person who coaches people
  through injury and disappointment — tone stays human, not clinical-cold.
- **Short sentences for headlines, longer for explanation.** Mirrors a coach
  talking through a plan: the headline is the instruction, the body is the
  "here's why."

Do: "Strength work isn't optional. It's the first thing that goes when
runners get busy — and the first thing that should come back."
Don't: "Unlock your BEST run ever with our AMAZING strength secrets!!!"

## Brand Personality

Disciplined · Grounded · Precise · Warm · Unhurried · Quietly confident.
Think a coach who has already raced the race you're training for and has
nothing left to prove — so there's no need to shout.

## 3. Visual System

### Color Palette

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--ink` | `#12151A` | Primary dark background, headline text on light |
| `--ink-soft` | `#1B1F26` | Secondary dark surface (cards on dark sections) |
| `--bone` | `#F5F1E8` | Primary light background (warm off-white, not clinical white) |
| `--paper` | `#FFFFFF` | Card surfaces on bone background |
| `--ember` | `#FF4E32` | Primary accent — CTAs, highlights, energy |
| `--ember-dark` | `#D93A22` | Ember hover/active state |
| `--moss` | `#33473C` | Secondary accent — recovery, outdoor, trust |
| `--moss-light` | `#7C9C82` | Tints, badges, subtle backgrounds |
| `--gray-900`–`--gray-50` | grayscale ramp | Body text, borders, muted UI |

Rationale: near-black + warm bone (not stark white) reads as premium and
editorial (Tracksmith), ember is the "high performance / energy" signal
(WHOOP/Strava territory without copying either), moss keeps the palette from
feeling like a generic fitness-red brand and nods to trail/outdoor culture
and to recovery/calm.

### Typography

- **Display / headlines:** `Oswald` (condensed, uppercase-friendly, track-
  and-field poster energy) — weights 500/600/700.
- **Body / UI:** `Inter` (already the house body font across CC-Studio
  client sites) — weights 400/500/600/700.

Large type, generous line-height on body copy, tight tracking removed on
headlines (Oswald is condensed enough on its own).

### Logo Direction

Wordmark-first: `GROUNDWORK` set in Oswald 600, all caps, normal tracking,
no additional mark required for v1. Optional mark: a single horizontal
ground-line with one upward stride/footstrike notch — literal "groundwork"
under a runner's foot. Kept as a CSS/SVG construction (no image asset) so it
stays crisp at any size and needs no binary files in the repo.

### Icon Style

Thin-line (1.5–2px stroke), rounded caps, monochrome — same feather-icon
convention as the Mustard Digital Marketing site, so the two client builds
share a technical approach even though the brand skins differ completely.

### Photography Style

Documentary, not staged: natural light, muted/desaturated color grade,
Toronto streets-meet-trail settings. Mix of wide shots (open road, trail,
track) and close texture shots (laces, tape, foam roller grain, chalked
hands on a barbell). No glossy stock-photo energy, no forced smiles at the
camera. (This build uses labeled placeholder blocks in place of real photo
assets — no binary images are committed.)

## 4. Audience

Runners at every level who train seriously enough to want to stay
uninjured: marathoners and half-marathoners, recreational and competitive
5K/10K runners, trail runners, busy professionals fitting training around a
career, and runners returning from injury who need a rebuild plan, not just
a return-to-run note.
