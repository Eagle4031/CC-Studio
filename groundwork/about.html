Meet Melika

An Unusual Path Into Running

Melika Ghali started running only about 18 months before landing on an NCAA Division I roster — training out of Toronto with the University of Toronto Track Club under coaches Terry Radchenko and Gabe Johnston. In that short window he represented Team Ontario at OFSAA and Cross Country Nationals, running personal bests of 3:49 for 1500m and 1:52 for 800m.

That trajectory carried him to Boise State University for track and cross country, then as a graduate transfer to Eastern Michigan University, where he completed a Master's in Exercise Physiology while racing distance events. In the 2018–19 season he earned Second Team All-MAC honors, placing 2nd in the 5000m (15:01) and 5th in the 10,000m (31:56.48) at the Mid-American Conference Championships.

After competing, Melika became a Registered Massage Therapist, building a practice around the running population he understood best — first through general clinical work, then specializing with runners at The Runner's Academy in Toronto. He combines deep tissue therapy, sports massage, myofascial work, joint mobilizations, and dynamic stretching with a coaching approach built on the same training principles that took him to Division I competition.

Outside the clinic, Melika mentors youth through the Taking Kids Out of Poverty (TKOP) program in Toronto's St. Jamestown community, supporting after-school literacy and numeracy work — and he's currently expanding his own scope through acupuncture certification.

Coaching Philosophy

Four Ideas Everything Is Built On

Recovery-First Performance

Adaptation happens after the session — recovery capacity is programmed, not squeezed in when there's time left over.

Strength As Foundation

Durable runners are built in the gym before they're built on the road. Strength work is treated as training, not cross-training.

Longevity Over Quick Fixes

The goal is decades of running. Programming is built to still be working in five years, not just five weeks.

Evidence-Based, Always

Decisions trace back to sports science and clinical assessment — not whatever training trend is popular this month.
